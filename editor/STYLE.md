# Editor style sheet — weaties.ghost.io

The standing rules for a proofreading pass on a Ghost draft. Read this before every pass. It exists so the editor stops re-guessing the author's preferences, and stops "fixing" things that are his voice.

This repo is shareable, so this file holds **rules and public proper nouns only**. No post content, and no names of private people.

## The job

**Proofread, never rewrite.** The author's words, order and phrasing stay exactly as written. Anything that would change wording goes into a comment for him to decide, not into the text.

## Fix silently, then list in the report

- Misspellings.
- Apostrophes: `it's` vs `its`, plurals written with an apostrophe (`Lot's`, `box's`), missing possessives (`peoples phones`).
- Wrong-word slips where the intended word is beyond doubt (`buy` for `by`).
- Capitalisation of proper nouns, and stray capitals on common nouns mid-sentence (`the Gas furnace`, `beautiful Kayaks`).
- A lowercase letter starting a sentence.
- A missing full stop at the end of a body paragraph.
- Hyphen in a compound number modifier (`6-mile hike`).
- One-word subject-verb agreement (`everyone … have had` to `has had`). **Always list these in the report as needing his eye**, since they edge toward grammar.
- Inconsistent capitalisation of the same name inside one post. Match the form used elsewhere in the post.

## Leave alone — this is his voice

- **Two spaces after a full stop.** Deliberate. Never normalise. Note that page-text extraction collapses them, so search contexts must not span a sentence break.
- Comma splices and long run-on sentences.
- A spaced hyphen used as a dash (`cool - but pricey`).
- Emoji, `;)`, exclamation marks.
- Casual grammar: `Me and my friend`, `who I used to work with`, `friends that are members`.
- `20's`, `Sept 11th`, `50%` and similar informal number and date forms.
- Sentence fragments as captions.
- Headings in sentence case or title case, whichever he used.

## Captions

- Much of the narrative lives in image captions. Proofread them like body text.
- A one-sentence or fragment caption may end without a full stop. Do not add one.
- A caption of two or more sentences gets a closing full stop if it lacks one.

## Comments

Ghost has no comment feature, so comments are **callout cards** whose text starts with `CLAUDE:` (chosen 2026-09-20 over inline brackets or a separate list). He deletes each one as he deals with it.

- One issue per callout, placed directly under the body paragraph or heading it concerns. A callout cannot be placed inside a caption, so anchor it to the nearest body paragraph above and say which caption it means.
- State the observation and why a reader might trip. **Do not draft replacement wording.**
- Keep it to two or three sentences.
- **On a repeat pass, never re-raise a comment he deleted without acting on.** That was his decision. Comment only on new text or new problems.

Worth a comment:

- A person, boat or organisation that appears with no introduction, or is introduced after its first use.
- A heading that promises something the section never mentions.
- Days and dates that disagree across sections.
- Body text and a nearby caption that tell slightly different stories.
- The same phrase repeated within a sentence or two.
- A section that is only a photo.
- Long narrative passages that are captions, which publish as small centred text.
- Naming a minor or another private third party. Flag once, neutrally, as his call.
- A feature image with no caption or alt text.
- The closing: a post that opens with a greeting and ends with no sign-off.

Not worth a comment: anything in "Leave alone" above, word choice, or sentence length.

## Never

- Never click **Publish**, **Update**, **Unpublish** or anything in post settings.
- **Never type into a post whose status is not `Draft`.** A published post is public content, and it may already have gone out by email. Read-only checks are fine. Edits need his explicit say-so in chat for that post.
- Never delete his text. Repair your own mistakes with undo, one step at a time.

## The report

End every pass with:

1. The count of callouts left in the post, and the heading each sits under, so he can confirm none remain before publishing.
2. Every fix made, grouped by kind.
3. Fixes that need his eye: agreement changes and any proper noun that was assumed.
4. Things noticed but deliberately not changed, such as empty paragraphs.
5. Any slip made during the pass and how it was repaired.
